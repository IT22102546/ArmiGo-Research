II.	LITERATURE REVIEW
A.	Wearable Sensor-Based Upper Limb Rehabilitation
        
		Hemiplegia can occur prior to or at the time of delivery and infants will normally be diagnosed as having hemiplegia when they are between four months and twelve months of age because of stroke or brain injury [1],[2] Hemiplegic children can also experience cognitive and speech delays in addition to neurodevelopmental comorbidity [3]. The ARMIGO glove is a rehabilitation device for children designed to help with rehabilitation of the upper limb through use of sensors placed at each of the four joints of the upper limb (fingers, wrist, elbow and shoulder). Unlike other devices such as haptic devices that provide feedback through force and/or vibration to assist with rehabilitation, the ARMIGO glove is focused on measuring joint movement and providing feedback through measurement of joint movement alone (i.e., there is no force applied to the user). Wearable Sensing Technologies, especially those using Inertial Measurement Units (MPU 9250) and Flex Sensors, are one of the best technologies to collect real-time kinematic data and rehabilitate children with hemiplegia [5],[6] but most current rehabilitation devices utilize the analysis of single joints rather than assessing the interaction of multiple joints [7].

B.	Virtual Reality and Gamification in Pediatric Rehabilitation.
 
 	Using Virtual Reality (VR) research-based applications do not only have entertainment-related uses, such as virtual games, gamified health solutions for treating common phobias [19], and prop-house-type virtual applications for finding relevant movie props [20], but also have the potential for numerous healthcare and related applications, such as cardiovascular training [21] and medically important plant identification [22]. Using virtual reality (VR) and serious gaming technologies as a method of rehabilitation for pediatric patients has been proven successful in surpassing the challenges of traditional rehabilitation methods, which often have difficulty keeping patients motivated [21],[16]. VR makes the repetitive exercises used in traditional rehabilitation methods more interesting, so they are delivered as game-based therapies, which can lead to improved patient attention and compliance [21],[8],[9]. Several studies have shown some level of benefit with the use of VR to facilitate recovery of upper limb function through goal-directed activities (i.e., functional movements) being incorporated into the gaming experience [8],[9]; the involvement of the parent(s) has been identified as a critical factor in adherence to at-home therapy activities [10]. However, the current VR systems being used primarily focus on facilitating gross motor movements and provide little ability to monitor the fine motor movements involved in an activity [19]. Other limitations of the current VR systems are that they do not provide emotional adaptations for the child [15], and they are prohibitively expensive because of the cost of the technology required to develop and implement VR systems [19]. Therefore, there is a strong need for a single comprehensive solution that utilizes immersive virtual reality technology and the latest smart motion validation and feedback technologies to optimize the rehabilitation of pediatric patients [19].   


C.	Machine Learning for Movement Recognition and Quality Assessment

The increasing application of machine learning for rehabilitation systems is aimed at classifying the type of rehabilitation motion and using motion data from sensor technologies. The traditional approaches are limited in terms of being able to classify upper extremity rehabilitation motions due to patient variability and errors in measurement [18]. Various machine learning techniques such as Support 
Vector Machines (SVMs), Random Forests, and Neural 
Networks were studied [18]. Specifically, deep learning with 
Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks have been shown to effectively classify complex patterns of movement across time in wearable sensor data [6][7]. Research on LSTMs has shown potential for improving the classification of movement patterns and the assessment of rehabilitation sessions for children with hemiplegia by analyzing joint angles and movement [18]. However, limitations of existing rehabilitation systems include the use of narrow data sets (i.e., most data collected comes from adult patients) and difficulty or challenges in integrating machine learning into real-time immersive environments for feedback purposes [18]. Addressing these limitations would improve intelligent rehabilitation systems for pediatric patients. 
 
 
D. Tele-Rehabilitation and Remote Monitoring Systems.
 
 	Tele-rehabilitation technologies have recently developed as a new method for therapist-led distance rehabilitation for patients via the Internet of Things (IoT), which has emerged with significant advancements in telemetry [7], [17]. Tele-rehabilitation systems with continuous measurement of movement for assessing patient outcomes based on overall treatment performance are proven to improve the quality of care provided to pediatric patients during rehabilitative treatments using movement analysis through web-based platforms to share information about patient progress between therapists and their patients using mobile apps [7]. Existing tele-rehabilitation products still have no integration of the monitoring portion of technology-integrated teaching tools within the tele-rehabilitation program and the initial rehabilitation therapy itself [30]. In addition, the availability of resources in developing nations has restricted many children with motor disabilities from accessing physiotherapy services and consequently affected the amount of time they can spend receiving the required rehabilitation [4].
